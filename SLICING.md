# Slicing the cam assembly in PrusaSlicer

How to slice the cam and gear so the surfaces that rub are solid, without paying for a solid part everywhere. Terms follow `CONTEXT.md`.

## What needs to be strong

- **Cam**: the lobes and troughs. The follower runs on the crests, ramp faces, drop faces and troughs, so the outer band of the plate takes all the wear. The middle of the plate and the hub carry little load.
- **Gear**: the teeth, and the plug. The teeth mesh with the drive; the plug carries the torque into the socket.

Print 1 used 2 perimeters and 15 percent grid infill. That leaves a 0.9 mm skin over sparse infill at every contact surface, which is why it will wear quickly.

## The two settings, and where to put them

**Fill density** does the structural job. At 100 percent the region is solid and there is no skin to wear through.

**Perimeters** is a refinement. Perimeter lines follow the outline, so the material directly behind a lobe face is continuous extrusion running along the face. Solid infill is laid in parallel lines at 45 degrees, so at the face it ends in short segments meeting the wall at an angle, and it can leave tiny voids in narrow corners. A few perimeters give a tougher, cleaner contact skin with no wall-to-infill seam, but once the infill is solid more than 3 or 4 adds little. On the gear more perimeters can be worse: the teeth are so narrow they become nothing but perimeter anyway.

Put them in different places:

- **Fill density = 100 percent on a modifier**, so only the contact band is solid. A modifier that changes only infill does not create extra perimeters, because regions with the same perimeter settings are merged when perimeters are generated.
- **Perimeters on the object**, not on a modifier. If the perimeter count is set on a modifier, the modifier becomes its own region and the slicer draws perimeters around its whole boundary, including the inner edge of the ring in the middle of the plate. On the object, the only boundaries are the real outline and the bore.

## Recommended settings

| Part | Object setting | Modifier | Modifier setting |
|---|---|---|---|
| cam | Perimeters 3 | `stl/dominator_cam_rim_modifier.stl` | Fill density 100 percent |
| gear | Perimeters 3 | `stl/dominator_gear_tooth_modifier.stl` and `stl/dominator_gear_plug_modifier.stl` | Fill density 100 percent |
| washer | Perimeters 3, 100 percent infill globally | none | |

If you prefer one number, leave both objects at 3 perimeters and let the modifiers do the work. Going to 6 perimeters on the cam is harmless but unnecessary once the rim band is solid.

Other settings worth using:

- Slow the external perimeter speed on the cam, since the lobe faces are the working surface.
- 0.12 to 0.2 mm layers, 0.4 mm nozzle. Finer layers give smoother tooth flanks.
- PETG, ASA or nylon. PLA goes soft in a hot pool box.
- No supports on any part in the exported orientations: cam plate down and hub up, gear flat side down and plug up.

## Steps in PrusaSlicer

1. Import the part STL.
2. Right click the part in the object list, Add settings, Layers and perimeters, set Perimeters.
3. Right click the part, Add modifier, Load, pick the modifier STL. It is exported on the same origin and in the same orientation as the part, so it lands in place. Do not move or scale it.
4. Right click the modifier, Add settings, Infill, set Fill density to 100 percent. Add nothing else to the modifier.
5. Repeat 3 and 4 for the second gear modifier.
6. Slice and check the preview: the rim band, teeth and plug should show solid infill, the rest of the plate your global infill, and there should be no ring of perimeters at the modifier's inner edge.

## What the modifiers cover

| Modifier | Covers | Config value |
|---|---|---|
| `dominator_cam_rim_modifier` | outer band of the plate from 8 mm inside the trough circle to beyond the crests, plus half a millimetre above and below the plate | `rim_band` |
| `dominator_gear_tooth_modifier` | the teeth and 4 mm inside the root circle, full gear thickness | `gear_band` |
| `dominator_gear_plug_modifier` | the plug and the gear body directly under it | |

They are OpenSCAD parts in `scad/assembly.scad`, regenerated by `render.ps1`, so they track any change to the geometry.
